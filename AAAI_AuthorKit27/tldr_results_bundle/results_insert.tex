\subsection{Main Results}

Across all four benchmarks, TLDR improves macro-F1 over the strongest prior
retriever by 2.83 points with ZH+EN training and by 8.95 points with ZH-only
training. It leads in seven of eight regime--dataset settings; the sole
exception is AISHELL1-NE under ZH-only training, where it trails CLAR by 0.1
point. This clean-speech result prevents an across-the-board claim, while the
overall pattern shows that TLDR's advantage grows as training coverage becomes
more restricted.

The restricted-training results in Figure~\ref{fig:main_results} isolate the
robustness gains. Relative to the strongest prior, TLDR gains 12.0 F1 points on
ContextASR-ZH, 7.4 on the unseen English ContextASR-EN subset, and 16.5 on the
cross-domain AISpeechMeeting benchmark. The much smaller changes on clean
AISHELL1-NE indicate that the main benefit is not uniform score inflation, but
better generalization under language or domain mismatch.

For downstream contextual ASR, TLDR obtains the lowest retrieval-based WER on
all four datasets. Relative to raw decoding, it reduces WER by 34.4\%--59.4\%,
demonstrating the large value of contextual retrieval itself. The stricter
comparison with the strongest competing retriever yields smaller but consistent
relative reductions of 1.5\%--10.3\%. Oracle hotwords are shown only as a
reference upper bound and are not treated as a competing retrieval method.
